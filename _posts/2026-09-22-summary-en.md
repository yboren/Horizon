## [DeepSeek Releases DSec Sandbox Platform Technical Report for AI Agent Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22978) ⭐️ 9.0/10

DeepSeek and Tsinghua University released the DSec technical report, detailing a high-performance sandbox infrastructure that supports 3 million instances daily. The platform provides unified SDK access to various backends, including containers and Firecracker microVMs, to facilitate large-scale AI agent training. DSec addresses critical infrastructure bottlenecks in AI agent training by decoupling stateful rollout execution from GPU training. Its high-concurrency management and resource optimization techniques provide a scalable blueprint for the future of autonomous agent research. The platform achieves a peak concurrency of over 380,000 instances and uses a 3FS distributed file system with EROFS images to reduce task completion time by 1.7x. Additionally, memory sharing and recycling mechanisms reduce peak memory usage by approximately 40%.

The article introduces the concept of 'spymarks,' a form of steganographic tracking used to embed invisible, machine-readable identifiers into digital content for surveillance and attribution purposes. This technique represents an evolution from traditional watermarking, focusing on tracking individual content consumption and distribution. This technology raises significant privacy concerns, as it could enable granular ad-tech tracking and surveillance without user consent. It challenges the current understanding of digital anonymity and content integrity in an era of pervasive data collection. Spymarks can be embedded into various media types, including text via word choice variations or images via pixel-level manipulation. Unlike traditional watermarks, which often assert ownership, spymarks are primarily designed for covert tracking and data extraction.

NASA has effectively abandoned its current Mars Sample Return (MSR) mission architecture due to ballooning costs that reached an estimated $11 billion and significant technical delays. The project, which aimed to bring Martian soil back to Earth, is no longer viable under its original design and timeline. This cancellation marks a major shift in space policy, highlighting the growing tension between traditional government-led aerospace projects and the potential for lower-cost private industry alternatives. It also reflects broader concerns about the sustainability of high-budget planetary science missions in the current fiscal environment. The mission faced criticism for relying on legacy rocket designs rather than leveraging modern, high-capacity launch vehicles like Starship or New Glenn. Critics noted the extreme cost-to-payload ratio, as the mission would have returned only about 1.1 pounds of material.

The mission architecture involved a complex sequence of landing, sample collection, and an Earth Return Orbiter to transport the materials back. This initiative was intended to provide definitive evidence regarding the history of water and potential past life on Mars.

## [TypeSafe AI Introduces Jev: A New Class of 'Decision Models'](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/21/jev/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

TypeSafe AI has launched Jev, a 'System One' model that accepts unstructured text inputs and outputs typed, probabilistic data such as confidence scores and categorical choices instead of generative text. It is designed to function as a high-speed, cost-effective API for classification and ranking tasks. This represents a shift from generative LLMs to specialized decision models that provide software-native, deterministic outputs, making them easier to integrate into production code. By eliminating the need for parsing unstructured text, these models offer a more reliable and efficient way to handle automated business logic. Jev supports three types of queries: 'Noul' (Bernoulli) for yes/no questions, choice selection, and numeric scoring, all returned as floating-point numbers. The service is priced solely on input tokens, making it significantly cheaper than traditional LLMs that charge for both input and output.

rss · Simon Willison · Sep 21, 23:09

**Background**: Most modern AI models are generative, meaning they predict the next token in a sequence to create human-like text, which often requires complex parsing to use in software. Jev is built on the concept of 'System One' thinking, which refers to fast, automatic, and intuitive processing, contrasting with the slower, more deliberate 'System Two' reasoning often associated with complex LLM chains.
