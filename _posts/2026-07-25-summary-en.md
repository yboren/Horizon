## [Postgres LISTEN/NOTIFY Scalability Re-evaluated](https://www.dbos.dev/blog/postgres-listen-notify-scalability) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The article demonstrates that PostgreSQL's LISTEN/NOTIFY mechanism can achieve significantly higher throughput than previously assumed when properly optimized. By implementing specific tuning strategies, the authors reached 60,000 events per second on a high-performance server. This finding challenges long-standing community assumptions that LISTEN/NOTIFY is inherently unscalable for high-load applications. It provides developers with a viable, lightweight alternative for building event-driven architectures directly within their existing PostgreSQL database. The experiments were conducted on a powerful machine with 96 vCPUs and 384 GB of RAM, highlighting that performance is heavily dependent on hardware resources and batching techniques. The results suggest that while throughput can be high, developers must carefully consider their specific scaling factors and hardware constraints.

**Background**: PostgreSQL's LISTEN/NOTIFY is a built-in inter-process communication mechanism that allows database clients to send and receive asynchronous notifications. It is commonly used for simple pub/sub tasks, but it has historically been criticized for performance bottlenecks when handling extremely high volumes of messages compared to dedicated message brokers like Redis or RabbitMQ.
