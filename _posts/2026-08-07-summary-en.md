The essay argues that as AI lowers the barrier to code generation, technical proficiency is becoming commoditized. Consequently, personal 'taste'—the ability to discern quality, design, and architectural integrity—is emerging as the primary differentiator for software engineers. This shift suggests that the value of human engineers is moving away from rote implementation toward high-level judgment and curation. It highlights a critical evolution in the industry where the ability to guide AI tools effectively depends on deep, human-centric intuition. The author posits that while LLMs can solve immediate, isolated problems, they often struggle to maintain coherence or signal-to-noise ratio in large-scale, multi-developer codebases. The lack of human judgment in AI-generated systems may lead to fragile or poorly designed software architectures.
