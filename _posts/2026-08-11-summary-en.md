## [Parametron: The 1954 Japanese Computing Innovation Beyond Tubes and Transistors](https://ethw.org/Milestones:Parametron,_1954) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The Parametron is a unique logic circuit element invented by Eiichi Goto in 1954 that utilizes magnetic resonance in ferrite cores to perform digital logic. It served as the foundation for early Japanese computers like the NEAC-1101, which was notable for its reliability and use of floating-point operations. This technology represents a significant alternative path in computing history, demonstrating that reliable digital logic could be achieved without the fragile vacuum tubes or early, expensive transistors of the era. It highlights the diversity of engineering approaches that existed before the industry standardized on silicon-based semiconductors. The Parametron operates on the principle of parametric excitation, where a resonant circuit with a nonlinear reactive element oscillates at half the driving frequency. These devices were highly stable and power-efficient, though they were eventually superseded by the rapid miniaturization and speed improvements of transistors.

hackernews · xeonmc · Aug 10, 10:29 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49241846)

**Background**: In the 1950s, the computing industry was searching for reliable alternatives to vacuum tubes, which were prone to failure and consumed significant power. The Parametron was a Japanese innovation that leveraged magnetic core technology, which was also being explored in the US through magnetic logic and amplifiers. This era saw various experimental computing architectures, including cryotrons and tunnel-diode logic, before silicon transistors became the dominant standard.
