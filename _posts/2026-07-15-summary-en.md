SRM-LoRA introduces a novel gradient update method for LoRA that uses a sensitivity-based Riemannian metric to suppress hallucination-prone parameter updates. This approach effectively improves factual reliability without adding any computational overhead during inference. Hallucinations remain a critical barrier to the reliable deployment of LLMs in real-world applications. By integrating mathematical theory into the optimization process, this method offers a more principled way to constrain model behavior and improve generalization. The method constructs a Riemannian metric based on the sensitivity of model parameters to loss signals, effectively acting as a brake on harmful updates. It was trained on the HaluEval-QA dataset and demonstrated improved performance on both related and out-of-distribution benchmarks.

Riemannian geometry is often used in machine learning to define the curvature of parameter spaces, allowing for more efficient optimization paths.
