## [ThinkingBox: Evaluating AI Agent Reliability via Stateful Workflow Consistency](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1x17shf/thinkingbox_solving_an_agent_task_once_vs_solving/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

ThinkingBox is a new benchmark that evaluates AI agents by running 507 stateful business workflows 20 times each to measure consistency, rather than relying on single-shot success metrics. It grades agents based on the final state of the backend database and side effects rather than just completion signals. This research highlights a critical reliability gap, showing that models often appear to succeed while leaving databases in incorrect states. By distinguishing between 'discovery' (solving once) and 'repeatability' (solving every time), it provides a more realistic metric for deploying AI agents in production environments. The benchmark includes 10,140 trials per model across five domains, revealing that rankings based on 'pass@1' often contradict those based on 'all-20' consistency. Many failures appear 'clean' to standard evaluators, despite the agent producing wrong field values or unintended side effects.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/tuhin_k · Oct 9, 00:50

**Background**: AI agents are software systems designed to perform multi-step tasks by interacting with tools and APIs to achieve a goal. In stateful workflows, the sequence of actions matters because each step changes the underlying system state. Current evaluation methods often focus on whether an agent claims to have finished a task, which can lead to overestimating reliability if the actual database state is not verified.

This discovery provides a rare opportunity to study stellar evolution in complex gravitational environments, offering new insights into how such unique triple systems form and evolve over time. The system features an inner orbital period of 8 days and an outer orbital period of 73.5 years, with findings published in The Astrophysical Journal Letters on October 9, 2026.

Pulsars are highly magnetized, rotating neutron stars that emit beams of electromagnetic radiation.
