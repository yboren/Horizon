A group of startup founders has petitioned the U.S. government to refrain from restricting access to Chinese open-weight AI models. This debate highlights the tension between national security concerns and the desire to maintain an open, competitive AI ecosystem. Restricting access to these models could lead to regulatory capture, favoring a few large U.S. companies while hindering smaller startups that rely on diverse model architectures. These technologies are central to the current debate over whether AI development should be centralized or remain open to global contributions.

## [Forensic Report Links Xiaomi SU7 Fatal Crash to Electronic Door Failure](https://t.me/zaihuapd/42732) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A forensic report on a 2025 Xiaomi SU7 crash confirms that a high-speed collision caused a short circuit, leading to a total low-voltage system power failure. This failure rendered the electronic door handles inoperable, preventing external rescue efforts. This incident highlights a critical safety design flaw in modern electric vehicles where reliance on electronic door releases can become a fatal trap during power-loss scenarios. It underscores the urgent need for reliable, mechanical emergency egress systems in all EVs. The vehicle was traveling at 167 km/h at the moment of impact, which crushed the battery cells and caused a surge that disabled the low-voltage electrical grid. Because the SU7 lacks external mechanical door handles, rescuers were unable to open the doors to reach the driver.

telegram · zaihuapd · Jul 24, 00:56

**Background**: Electric vehicles rely on a 12V low-voltage system to power essential components like door locks, sensors, and computers, separate from the high-voltage battery pack. Many modern EVs have replaced traditional mechanical handles with electronic buttons or flush-mounted handles to improve aerodynamics and aesthetics. However, if a crash severs the connection to the 12V system, these electronic mechanisms may fail, potentially trapping occupants inside.
